## In plain English

Eli Lilly's weight-loss pill Foundayo — the first oral competitor to Novo Nordisk's Wegovy pill — has been shipping from US pharmacies since April 6. Both are priced at $149 a month without insurance.

Legal analysts are now flagging that the FDA's recent removal of 12 experimental drugs called peptides (short protein fragments used for recovery and longevity) from its restricted list does not actually authorize specialty pharmacies to make them. The July 23 advisory meeting remains the gate.

In pet health, a company called Loyal just cleared two of three major FDA hurdles for a daily pill designed to extend dogs' lifespans — the first drug of its kind, possibly on the market by year's end. Two other companies are testing weight-loss drugs for overweight cats. Quiet day otherwise.

### Augur picks

**Loyal LOY-002 clears two of three FDA hurdles for a first-ever dog longevity drug.** The FDA's Center for Veterinary Medicine accepted both the Target Animal Safety and preliminary efficacy (RXE) sections of Loyal's expanded conditional approval application. The STAY trial — 1,300 dogs across 72 clinics — is the largest veterinary clinical trial ever conducted. If the final manufacturing element clears, this could reach market before year-end. Pet health is Ring 1. The Compound has no pet health property. This is a demand-side leading indicator.

**Cat 2 ≠ Cat 1 gap may be a legal trap.** Multiple law firm analyses (Orrick, National Law Review, FDA Law Blog) are publishing client alerts warning that compounders who begin compounding removed-Cat-2 peptides before Cat 1 placement face enforcement risk. If a compounder acts prematurely and draws FDA enforcement, it becomes a safety-incident catalyst.
