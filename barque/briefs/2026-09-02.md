## Summary

A US government programme letting people on Medicare — the health scheme most Americans join at 65 — buy weight-loss drugs like Ozempic (called GLP-1s) for $50 a month has now run two months. Headlines this week say 600,000 people signed up. We went looking for where that number came from and could not find a source anywhere.

What we could verify is smaller and messier. CVS and Walgreens each say they filled around 100,000 prescriptions. The head of the US Medicare agency said in a video on 29 July that 250,000 people had signed up, without saying whether their applications were approved. The agency itself has published no figure at all.

Prescriptions filled, sign-ups claimed and people actually enrolled are three different things being reported as one. We moved our forecast on the two numbers we can stand behind.

## Signal detail

**`medicare-glp1-bridge-uptake`: 0.63 → 0.55.** The first CMS-attributed sign-up figure this row has ever had, and it arrived with a contradiction attached.

*Historian.* The reference class is thin: Bridge is a Section 402 demonstration, not a benefit expansion. The nearest analogues (Part D 2006, the 2023 insulin cap) enrolled automatically or through the plan; Bridge needs a per-patient clinical attestation, and year-one participation under that friction runs 10–15%. Atypical in one direction that matters: demand is already maxed and the barrier was price alone.

*Skeptic.* The falsifier — a credible trajectory above 1,000,000 by 31 December — has partially materialised. 250,000 at day 29 against KFF's verified 3.8M eligible is ~6.6% of the pool in a month; held linearly, six months clears the bar. The counter is that every reachable number counts the wrong thing.

*Bayesian.* Prior 0.63. Strongest new evidence: Oz's 250,000 (X, 2026-07-29) and CVS's >100,000 fills as of mid-August, both read at source in NPR (Sydney Lupkin, 2026-08-26). Against: NPR states plainly it is unclear whether those prior authorisations were approved, and CMS declined to give NPR any figure at all. Posterior **0.55**. A linear hold clears 1M; any normal decay curve does not, and that straddle is why the move is −0.08 and not −0.15.

*Augur.* Buried in the same piece: Walgreens' chief pharmacy officer says **around half** of its Bridge patients had never taken a GLP-1 before. A 65-plus, GLP-1-naive cohort arriving at scale, without the vocabulary our audience has, is a genuinely new reader — and the only fully monetisable thing in today's signal set. Filed as a work order.
