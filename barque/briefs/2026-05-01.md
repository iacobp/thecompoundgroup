## In plain English

The government panel that will decide whether experimental drugs called peptides (short protein fragments used for recovery and longevity) can be made at specialty pharmacies has only 3 of its 12 voting seats filled — and no chair. Health Secretary Kennedy has been filling similar vacancies on other advisory boards, including the vaccine committee back in February, but hasn't touched this one in nine weeks. That panel meets July 23 to decide the fate of BPC-157 and six other peptides. The fewer independent voices in the room, the harder the outcome is to predict.

Separately, the Trump administration quietly canceled its more ambitious plan for covering weight-loss drugs like Ozempic under Medicare. Insurers pushed back. The simpler backup program — a $50 monthly copay — still launches July 1 as the only option for seniors.

Meanwhile, Scientific American, STAT News, and academic journals are all publishing deep examinations of the peptide trend. The investigative scaffolding for a safety-focused story is being built piece by piece.

Quiet day on price movements. Two forecasts shifted modestly.
