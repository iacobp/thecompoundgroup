The distinction matters: if it's a biologic, specialty pharmacies would never be able to make cheaper copies. The Medicare $50-a-month weight-loss drug program passed day 10 without incident.

### Augur picks

**Lilly retatrutide biologic classification lawsuit — compounding precedent.** A federal judge ruled the FDA "acted unlawfully" by arbitrarily determining retatrutide was not "analogous to a protein," sending the classification back on remand. Lilly has also appealed, seeking full biologic reclassification. If retatrutide is ultimately classified as a biologic, it gets 12 years of market exclusivity and cannot be compounded under 503A/503B during shortages. If it stays classified as a drug, compounders could make generic copies during any future shortage — replaying the semaglutide boom/bust cycle. This is the first time a major pharma company has proactively sued to prevent compounding access to a GLP-1-class drug *before* the drug is even approved. NDA expected Q4 2026, approval ~late 2027. The legal outcome will set precedent for the entire compounding ecosystem.
