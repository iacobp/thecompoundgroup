## In plain English

The FDA proposed a rule to permanently bar large-scale compounding pharmacies from making copycat versions of weight-loss drugs like Ozempic (called GLP-1s). The proposal covers semaglutide, tirzepatide (the ingredient in Zepbound), and liraglutide. Public comments are open through June 29. This is separate from the July 23 panel meeting about peptides (experimental drugs used for recovery and longevity) — different drugs, different regulatory pathway.

Akston Biosciences, which is running a weekly GLP-1 injection trial for cats at Cornell, has filed for a $20 million IPO. A pet-drug company going public is a bet that pet obesity treatment becomes a real market, not just a research curiosity.

The national estrogen patch shortage is worsening. Prescriptions have doubled since 2023 after the FDA removed safety warnings from hormone therapy last year. Manufacturers say the shortage could last three years.

No forecast probabilities moved today.

## How Barque got smarter today

- **503B vs 503A distinction matters for forecasting.** The FDA 503B GLP-1 exclusion targets outsourcing facilities (mass compounding), not traditional 503A pharmacies (individual prescriptions). Prior Ra briefs sometimes conflated the two. Future references should specify which pathway is affected — critical for peptide forecasts because PCAC reclassification primarily enables 503A compounding.
