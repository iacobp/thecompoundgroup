## In plain English

Eli Lilly's new weight-loss pill Foundayo filled just 3,707 prescriptions in its second week on the market — compared with about 18,400 for Novo Nordisk's rival pill, oral Wegovy, in the same period. Lilly's stock fell on the data. For anyone watching the weight-loss drug race, Novo is winning the oral pill battle so far, even though Foundayo can be taken any time of day without fasting.

No new signals on the FDA's peptide advisory panel, which meets in 86 days with six empty seats still unfilled. Hims & Hers earnings on May 11 remain the next genuine test of whether the company's 49% stock rally is built on revenue or hope. On the pet side, a dog longevity startup called Loyal has now cleared two of three FDA hurdles — the farthest any dog lifespan-extension drug has ever gone. Quiet Monday otherwise.

### Direct updates

All seven pending forecasts hold today. No probability moved. This is appropriate for a Monday following a thorough Sunday weekly review with no material weekend evidence.

**Foundayo slow launch (Ring 1 — GLP-1 metabolic, no active forecast):** IQVIA Week 2 data shows Foundayo at 3,707 prescriptions versus Wegovy oral at 18,410 — a roughly 5x gap. Week 1 was a partial period (1,390 Rx over 2 days via LillyDirect only). Analysts had expected roughly 5.4 million prescriptions by year-end to hit the $1.7B 2026 consensus. The gap is driven by Wegovy's first-mover advantage in oral GLP-1 (January launch vs. Foundayo's April 6), established Novo prescriber relationships, and potential formulary access lag. LLY declined on the data; NVO rose. For GLP-1 Picks, this is the first empirical head-to-head data worth publishing. For the Medicare Bridge forecast: Foundayo is one of three eligible drugs — slow retail uptake could mean either pent-up demand waiting for the Bridge's $50 copay or general adoption friction. Net neutral on probability.

**LOY-002 dog longevity (Ring 1 — pet health, no active forecast):** Loyal's LOY-002 received FDA acceptance of its Target Animal Safety section in January 2026, following Reasonable Expectation of Effectiveness acceptance in February 2025. Two of three required technical sections for conditional approval are now complete. The STAY study — 1,300 dogs across 70 clinics, the largest veterinary clinical trial in history — completed enrollment in July 2025. Only the manufacturing section remains. Conditional approval application expected 2027. This strengthens the dog-longevity-tracker opportunity in the pipeline but the original trigger (manufacturing acceptance) has not yet fired.

**Akston AKS-562c Cornell cat trial (Ring 1 — pet health):** The 11-week trial at Cornell (70 cats, once-weekly GLP-1 Fc-fusion injection) started late November 2025. By timeline, initial results should be completing now. No published results yet. Preclinical data showed no adverse effects and confirmed once-weekly dosing pharmacokinetics. Results would be material for glp1pets and could trigger a pet-health forecast candidate.
